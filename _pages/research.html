Research

Physics models are trustworthy but slow; AI surrogates are fast but hard to trust. My research connects the two. Physics-informed deep learning builds mechanics into the AI model, uncertainty quantification measures how much each prediction can be trusted, and agentic AI automates the analysis by choosing a model, running it, and checking the result.

1. Physics-informed AI

Embedding physics for trustworthy response prediction

Physical information injected into deep learning makes structural response predictions trustworthy. The physics can be encoded in the architecture, imposed through the loss, or used to guide the data.

Neural network–augmented physics model (NNAP-m)

A physics model in modal coordinates carries the global dynamics with only a few modes. A neural network learns the uncertain, response-dependent forces. Together they give the dynamic response of all degrees of freedom. The model was verified on the Lysefjord suspension bridge under wind loads.

Jeon, J., and J. Song (2025). Journal of Engineering Mechanics, 151(1), 04024103. [Paper]

Unified hysteresis model via physics-based deep learning

One deep learning model represents diverse hysteresis. It combines three physics-based ingredients and was validated on yielding-link tests:

  • a physics-encoded architecture, in which an LSTM cell emulates the Bouc–Wen solution process;
  • a physics-informed loss that keeps energy dissipation non-negative (Drucker’s postulate);
  • data augmentation that resamples hysteresis data, using rate independence.

Jeon, J., O. Kwon, and J. Song (2026). Earthquake Engineering & Structural Dynamics, 55(2), 379–396. [Paper] [Code]

2. Uncertainty quantification

Decomposing the uncertainty of probabilistic surrogate models

Key question. Is the uncertainty in a predicted structural response due to a lack of data, or is it irreducible even with more data?

A single surrogate model cannot separate model-form error from inherent data noise: its aleatoric estimate also absorbs model bias and sampling effects. I instead train an ensemble of Bayesian neural networks with diverse architectures, independently. The epistemic part (variance, discrepancy and bias) is what more data or better models can reduce; the aleatoric part is taken as the minimum over the ensemble.

The result is a tighter bound on inherent randomness, and an epistemic measure of what more data or better models can reduce, shown for hysteretic force predictions under stochastic ground motions.

Jeon, J., J. Song, and O. Kwon (2026). Structural Safety, 121, 102698. [Paper]

3. Analysis automation

Multi-agent LLM framework for structural reliability analysis

A problem stated in plain language becomes an interpreted failure probability Pf. LLM agents reason; validated solvers compute. The framework runs locally on open-weight models through a chat-based web interface.

  1. User states the problem in plain language.
  2. Problem Analyst turns the text into a problem specification and asks for missing inputs.
  3. Method Planner, a QLoRA fine-tuned model, selects the analysis method; the user confirms it.
  4. Code Engineer writes the solver script.
  5. Runner executes validated, non-LLM solvers.
  6. Result Interpreter explains the engineering meaning and offers refinement.
  7. Output: Pf with its interpretation.
